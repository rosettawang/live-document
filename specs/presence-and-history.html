Who's here, and what just changed

Status: open open · created Aug 5, 2026 · priority 50

A document that rewrites itself needs to answer two questions at a glance: who else is looking at this right now, and what changed since I last did.

Shipped with the core, because the core is unsafe without it

ThingWhere it isWhy it couldn't wait
Presence — "with Kenya, Ed" Status bar, bottom left Knowing someone else is in the document changes how you edit. It is also the cheapest possible version: names of open sockets, no cursors, no avatars.
Attribution — a name in the block's top-right on hover, violet for Claude Every block When a rewrite is wrong, the first thing anyone needs to know is whether a person wrote that sentence or a model did, before deciding whether to trust the price in it.
Undo — one button, reverses the last change by anyone Status bar; also the toast after a delete This is what makes it safe to let an AI rewrite a block with no confirm step. Without it, every AI edit needs an accept/reject gate, which trains people to click accept.
Connection dot — green live, coral reconnecting Status bar Typing into a document that is quietly disconnected is the worst-feeling bug available here.

Verified Aug 5, 2026: two browser tabs, edit in one, appears in the other in about a second; Undo restored the original text in both.

What's left

1. A history anyone can read

Every mutation already writes a history row — block, previous html, kind, timestamp, who — so the data is there and nothing surfaces it. What's missing is a panel: "Kenya changed the Flights table · 14:20 · show what changed", newest first, with a revert on each entry.

The reason this matters more than it sounds: people will come back to this document after not looking at it for a day, and "what did everyone do while I was asleep" has no answer right now beyond reading the whole page. On a trip with a five-hour time difference between Reykjavík and the US east coast, that is most mornings.

2. Undo mine, not undo theirs

Undo currently pops the global history stack. At four people that is usually right and occasionally startling — Ben presses Undo meaning "the thing I just did" and reverses Kenya's edit from ten seconds earlier. The fix is a per-person cursor: walk back to the most recent entry where by = you. Cheap; just not done.

3. Redo

Undo deletes the history row it consumed, so there is nothing to redo from. A kind='undo' entry recording what was reversed would give redo for free — and would also stop repeated Undo presses from silently walking back further than anyone intended, which is the more likely real complaint.

Rejected: live cursors and per-character presencethe thing collaborative editors have

Coloured carets showing exactly where each person is typing. It is the demo everyone recognises, and it needs per-keystroke position broadcasting — which the block model deliberately does not have (live-doc-core) and which would put a redraw under someone's cursor every time a teammate moves.

What it actually buys at four people editing a nine-section trip plan, mostly at different hours in different countries, is nearly nothing: the collision it prevents — two people in the same paragraph in the same second — is already handled by the keep-mine/keep-theirs prompt, and handled honestly.

Verification for what's left

  1. Make three changes as three different names; open the history panel and confirm all three, newest first, with the right names.
  2. Revert the middle entry and confirm the other two survive.
  3. Press Undo as Ben after Kenya edited more recently; confirm Ben's change reverses, not Kenya's.
